# Manufacturing-Construction-Risk-Allocation-in-Large-Power-System-Projects
A lifecycle-based assessment of prefabrication, site-based assembly, and hybrid delivery strategies for electrical infrastructure.

How can power system project teams optimize the allocation of manufacturing and construction activities to reduce lifecycle risk, schedule uncertainty, cost overruns, quality defects, and interface failures?

I would classify three main concepts that would be used:
1- Factory-manufactured equipment
Equipment such as switchgear panels, protection relays, control panels, transformers, and circuit breakers is manufactured or assembled in a factory and delivered to the project site.

2- Site-built or site-integrated infrastructure
Grounding grids, cable trenches, duct banks, building works, structural support, and electrical connections are constructed or completed on site.

3- Hybrid or integrated prefabrication
E-houses, packaged substations, skid-mounted electrical systems, and preassembled control systems combine equipment and interfaces into larger assemblies.

My study will classify components along separate dimensions as:
- Where it is manufactured or assembled: factory, site, or both.
- How it is delivered: individual equipment, skid, package, or complete module.
- Where it is integrated and tested: factory, site, or both.
- How much customization is required: standardized, configurable, or project-specific.

The lifecycle model is about tracking each component from the first procurement decision through final handover as follows:
1- Design and engineering (Requirements, specifications, drawings, design freeze and changes)
2- Raw materials and procurement (Supplier capacity, material availability, lead times, traceability, and price volatility)
3- Manufacturing or construction (Labor productivity, factory capacity, weather exposure, workmanship, rework, and quality assurance)
4- Factory testing and acceptance (Inspection, routine tests, documentation, nonconformities, release for shipment)
5- Transport and site readiness (Packaging, shipping damage, customs, lifting access, foundations, storage, delivery sequencing)
6- Installation and integration (Mechanical and electrical connections, cable termination, alignment, protection settings, and coordination)
7- Commissioning and handover (Site acceptance tests, integrated functional tests, energization, documentation, and defects)
8- Operation and Maintenance (Reliability, spare parts, maintainability, warranty, and lifecycle support)
